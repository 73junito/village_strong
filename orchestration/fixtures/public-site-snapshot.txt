Fathers Advancing Meaningful Identity, Leadership & Youth
Fatherhood learning designed around family connection.
FAMILY welcomes adult expectant, resident, nonresident, married, single, separated, returning, and otherwise disengaged fathers. Planned activities include peer discussion, fatherhood education, personal action planning, and resource navigation. These goals describe work under development, not demonstrated program outcomes.

FAMILY participation model
How the proposed Kentucky pilot would work.
FAMILY is planning a fathers-first hybrid model with live virtual participation, telephone access, regional in-person engagement, and supplemental self-paced materials.
Proposed Process - Enrollment Not Open
Five proposed steps
From initial inquiry to a personal engagement plan.
1 Express interest - Use the general contact channel without sending custody, clinical, child, or court-case details.
2 Eligibility and orientation - If a pilot is authorized, staff will explain fit, expectations, accessibility, confidentiality limits, and voluntary participation.
3 Join a live cohort - The proposed model uses closed, founder-led virtual groups with phone access and a regional in-person option.
4 Use optional materials - Original or permission-cleared reflections, audio, and short learning resources may supplement live sessions.
5 Create an engagement plan - Participants may identify safe, realistic goals and revisit them during optional follow-up.
What is known and what remains undecided.
Is enrollment open? No. The Kentucky pilot is still in development, and no cohort, schedule, or launch date has been approved.
What will it cost? Cost and funding have not been determined. No tuition or educational fee is currently being collected.
Do I need a computer? The proposed model includes mobile, telephone, printable, and low-bandwidth options, subject to final capacity.
Is FAMILY therapy or legal help? No. FAMILY is proposed as nonclinical fatherhood engagement and resource navigation, not therapy, diagnosis, legal representation, or custody evaluation.
Are referrals available? Future resource navigation may connect participants to independent qualified providers. No referral partnership is represented as active until an agreement exists.
How will privacy work? A future intake process will collect only necessary information through a protected system. Research consent, if ever offered, will remain separate from program participation.
No enrollment or sensitive intake is available on this website.
General questions may be sent through the contact page. Do not send emergency, clinical, custody, child, or court-case information.

=== /research ===
Fatherhood Research & Evaluation
Advancing Understanding of Fatherhood.
Our proposed research and evaluation work seeks to better understand paternal adjustment, father well-being, and experiences across the transition to and practice of fatherhood.
Research and instruments in development
No validated measures or study findings are currently claimed.
The concepts below are developmental. They are not diagnostic or clinical instruments, and no effectiveness claims or completed research outcomes are available.
Research vision
Questions before conclusions.
Future work may examine paternal identity, adjustment, emotional well-being, father-child relationships, family engagement, and the implementation of developing programs.
Pilot evaluation is a future goal, not a completed study. Research recruitment will not begin without an appropriate protocol, informed consent, privacy protections, and ethics review.
Concepts under development.
FRI - A proposed fatherhood-related measurement concept under development.
EFAS - A proposed concept for exploring experiences of paternal adjustment.
PPARS - A proposed concept related to paternal well-being and adjustment research.
A staged development and validation process.
The proposed FRI, PPARS, and EFAS instruments will move through the following stages before any score interpretation or public-use claim is considered established.
1 Construct development - Define each construct through literature review, father and caregiver perspectives, and clear boundaries between related concepts.
2 Item development - Create broad draft item pools, review reading level and inclusivity, and document the rationale for each proposed item.
3 Expert content review - Invite qualified experts and people with relevant lived experience to evaluate relevance, clarity, cultural fit, and domain alignment.
4 Cognitive interviews - Examine how fathers understand each item, choose responses, and experience wording before any larger pilot study.
5 Pilot administration - Use an approved protocol, informed consent, privacy safeguards, and de-identified data to examine feasibility and item performance.
6 Psychometric validation - Evaluate factor structure, reliability, convergent and discriminant validity, measurement invariance, and responsiveness in appropriate samples.
7 Governance and dissemination - Publish transparent scoring guidance, ethical-use limits, revision history, and evidence only after validation supports interpretation.
Research ethics
Privacy, consent, and appropriate review come first.
General website inquiries should not include sensitive family histories, children's information, clinical details, or research responses.
Village Strong Foundation will distinguish educational inquiry, program-interest communication, and research participation before collecting data for any future study.
No participant information or research responses are collected through this public page.
